# Employee Attrition Analysis Report

## Overview
This report analyzes employee attrition data for a company to identify hidden human resource management issues. The analysis was conducted using R programming, focusing on gender ratios, voluntary separations, department headcount crises, position vacancies, generational differences, and annual hiring/termination trends.

## Key Findings

### Gender Analysis
- **Gender Ratio**: Historically more female employees, but currently more male employees due to higher female attrition.
- **Attrition Reasons**: 
  - Females: 65% retire, 23% resign, 12% laid off.
  - Males: 52% retire, 31% resign, 18% laid off.
- **Retirement Age**: Females retire at 65, males at 60.
- **Department Imbalance**: IT department has only males; Accounting has only females.

### Voluntary Separations
- **Primary Reasons**: 69.8% retire, 30.2% resign.
- **Departments with High Attrition**: Produce, Meats, and Customer Service.
- **Age Group**: Highest attrition between ages 56–65.

### Department Headcount Crisis
- **Vacant Departments**: 12 departments (e.g., Training, Legal, IT, Accounting) currently have zero employees.
- **Aging Workforce**: Executive, Meats, and Store Management departments have employees with an average age of 50+.

### Position Headcount Crisis
- **Vacant Positions**: 24 out of 47 positions are unstaffed.
- **Cashier Position**: High turnover among employees aged 21–25, with an average tenure of 3 years.

### Generational Differences
- **Hiring Trends**: Most employees born in the 1980s; fewest in the 1990s.
- **Job Stability**: Employees born in the 1950s tend to stay longest (12+ years), while those born in the 1970s–1990s leave sooner.

### Annual Trends
- **Hiring Peaks**: Highest in 1998 due to expansion in Vancouver.
- **Attrition Peaks**: 
  - 2014 saw a sharp rise in layoffs (56.3% of terminations).
  - Voluntary separations peaked in 2008 (retirements) and 2012 (resignations).

## Impact
- **Gender Imbalance**: Limits diversity and innovation in departments like IT and Accounting.
- **Aging Workforce**: Pending retirements may lead to skill shortages.
- **High Attrition**: Labor-intensive departments (e.g., Customer Service) face constant turnover.

## Solutions
1. **Recruitment**: Target gender diversity in imbalanced departments (e.g., hire females in IT, males in Accounting).
2. **Succession Planning**: Proactively recruit younger employees in departments with aging staff.
3. **Retention Strategies**: Improve salary packages and work conditions for high-turnover roles (e.g., Cashiers).
4. **Data-Driven Hiring**: Align recruitment with historical trends to anticipate future needs.

## Tools Used
- **R Packages**: `ggplot2`, `dplyr`, `lubridate`.
- **Visualizations**: Bar plots, pie charts, line graphs, lollipop charts.

## Conclusion
The analysis highlights critical HR challenges, including gender disparities, aging workforce risks, and high attrition in key roles. Addressing these issues through targeted recruitment and retention strategies will ensure sustainable growth for the company.
